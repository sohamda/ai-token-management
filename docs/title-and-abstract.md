## Abstract (~140 words — fits a typical CFP or internal calendar invite)

> AI coding assistants are now a daily tool, but most teams use them like a free buffet — pasting whole repos, leaving giant chats running for days, and reflexively reaching for the most expensive model. The result is slow responses, blown premium-request budgets, and — counterintuitively — *worse* code, because models lose accuracy as their context fills up ("context rot").
>
> This session is a practical guide to using AI assistants with discipline. We'll cover the eight levers that actually move the needle: context engineering, prompt caching, tool design, custom instructions and skills, model routing, output discipline, repo hygiene, and workflow patterns including the **Ralph Wiggum loop**, **auto-compact**, and **agent handover**. Live demos and a token-cost calculator will show the impact in real numbers. You'll leave with a checklist your team can apply this week.

## Short version (~50 words — for chat / Slack invite)

> Most teams use AI coding assistants like a free buffet and pay for it in cost, latency, and quality. This session shows the eight levers — context engineering, caching, instructions, model routing, and the Ralph loop / auto-compact / handover patterns — that make AI workloads fast, cheap, and good.
